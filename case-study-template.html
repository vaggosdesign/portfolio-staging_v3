01

Intro

What is Sedex?

A global leader in supply chain data exchange, empowering businesses to make responsible and ethical decisions by providing transparency into supplier networks.

*Environmental, Social and Governance

What is the problem?

Our old tool called Radar produced inherent risk analysis for companies, serving as a predictive tool to help large organisations prioritise who to assess. Radar lived in a separate platform and its design language was completely different from the rest of Sedex’s toolkit.

What about the dashboard?

As we upgraded the platform’s design language, we also needed to update the dashboard. Because different widgets would appear on it, the designers came together and designed widgets using the knowledge from their focused teams. The opportunity was to incorporate Radar into the new dashboard as well.

Is there a future scope?

Because I was the designer on the supply-chain project, I took the initiative to bring Radar in as a separate effort. I designed the hierarchical visualisation of trade relationships first, and Radar’s prioritisation patterns and users’ map interactions would help us understand whether a future map-based supply-chain view could work.

02

Behind the scenes

What does Radar do?

The problem was first highlighted to our customer support department, with the majority of our major customers raising concerns about supplier-network transparency on the platform.

How does it work?

I joined a large workshop with field experts and the developers who built the old Radar to understand how it worked.

The platform uses an engine that understands patterns from risks uncovered by Sedex’s assessment tools and generates a risk prediction, known as inherent risk in the industry.

Is it worth investing time to change this tool?

This question was important so we could determine whether to shut the tool down, leave it as it was, or bring it into the new dashboard. After looking at the numbers, we decided it was worth assigning a small team, including myself, a Tech Lead, a Solutions Architect and a Front-End Developer, to connect Radar with the new dashboard.

What do users tell us?

At this stage, I conducted several user research sessions. Customers said they used Radar to prioritise assessment requests, but I observed significant usability issues. Further analysis showed that interpreting the data was time-consuming and difficult to grasp quickly.

Users wanted Radar to live in the same platform they used to assess suppliers and analyse assessment data.

Google Analytics

The data showed that users interacted with the map most on this page. The hierarchy and filters therefore became secondary to the map experience.

Who uses Radar?

Radar began as an internal tool for Sedex’s ESG consulting team and was later released to customers as a standalone product alongside the main platform. It is used by Ethical Trade Coordinators, Health & Safety Officers and similar roles at companies such as Tesco, M&S, Walmart and Unilever. In Product, we defined a vision to bring the Radar interface and experience into the new dashboard.

The making

What is the tool hierarchy?

I produced a flowchart to clarify the hierarchical site structure and define the features for the new Radar.

First, what will the dashboard look like?

We introduced a bi-weekly Design Day, where the design team co-created the new dashboard. With clear ownership of different tools, each designer defined the widgets linked to their area.

What will Radar look like?

Using an AI assistant linked to my AI notetaker, I distilled my research and created initial design concepts for Radar on the new dashboard.

How do the prototypes perform?

After testing mid-fidelity wireframes, I prototyped the high-fidelity direction and tested it to validate what could become the final concept.

Moderated testing

All existing customers who participated said the new Radar would make their working lives easier.

Unmoderated testing

04

Final design

What does Radar do?

Radar helps enterprise Trade Coordinators prioritise which suppliers to assess first. When suppliers register their sites on Sedex, they inherit baseline risk informed by assessments of similar suppliers. Sedex’s AI then analyses assessment data and predicts which suppliers most require attention, giving coordinators a clear, actionable view.

New dashboard

A unified, lightweight overview of supply-chain health, with Radar integrated in the side navigation and a consistent visual language for seamless, at-a-glance insight.

New Radar

The core risk tools users rely on a world map, actionable Latest Risks widget and category breakdown, were refined for faster prioritisation and everyday decision-making.

06

Takeaways

What did I learn?

01

Leading without a PM

How to bridge business goals and user needs, frame the problem, and facilitate the team to co-define scope and success so we moved fast with shared clarity.

02

Small-team velocity

How to ship in short cycles with lightweight rituals, balancing speed and quality without creating delivery debt.

03

Coordinating designers at pace

How to set clear patterns, run focused critiques and unblock quickly so the dashboard stayed consistent while work progressed in parallel.

04

Enterprise-first thinking

How to prioritise high-signal KPIs, auditability and immediate actions that fit enterprise workflows, not just polished visuals.

05

From overview to action

How to connect a health overview to prioritised, in-context actions through Radar, reducing friction between spotting a risk and acting on it.

06

Explainability builds trust

How to embed model explainers, decision logs and a shared glossary so stakeholders understood why something was risky, increasing adoption.

07

Measure what matters

How to define and instrument practical outcomes, time to insight, throughput and hit rate, so we could learn, iterate and prove impact.
